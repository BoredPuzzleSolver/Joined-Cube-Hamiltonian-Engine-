# Independent audit: Space, mathematical reality, and proof

**Result:** No substantive physics or logic error found in outputs/Space_Mathematical_Reality_and_Proof.md. No edits were made to that document.

## Normalization and limits

The description correctly separates the graph construction, physical lattice spacing, spin cutoff and continuum limit. Setting \(\kappa=1\) fixes an energy unit for the reported dimensionless matrices; the ratio \(\nu/\kappa\) specifies their relative magnetic strength. It does not determine a physical value of \(a\). The report introduces no convention-dependent formula relating these coefficients to a bare coupling, so it introduces no factor-of-two normalization claim.

The statement about a dimensionless energy tending to zero while a physical mass stays positive is valid, for example for \(a\Delta_{\rm physical}\). It does not assert that every arbitrarily normalized matrix gap must have that behavior. Fixed-volume refinement and fixed-spacing volume growth are correctly distinguished.

[Jaffe–Witten, section 4](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf) requires a nontrivial theory on \(\mathbb R^4\), for any compact simple gauge group, satisfying the stated axiomatic properties and a positive spectral gap. The report does not substitute an ontology-of-space problem for that requirement.

## Gödel and spectral undecidability

The consistency assumption is correct for the modern Gödel–Rosser formulation. The intended meaning of “strong enough to express elementary arithmetic” must include enough arithmetic to represent the relevant computations; a standard precise sufficient hypothesis is a consistent recursively enumerable extension of Robinson arithmetic. Merely possessing arithmetic symbols would not suffice. The existing wording is conventional explanatory shorthand, not a claim about weak decidable arithmetic.

[Rosser's primary article extract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-symbolic-logic/article/abs/extensions-of-some-theorems-of-godel-and-church/0461E34DC1F219C459EE84CC2FA89068) explicitly distinguishes ordinary consistency from omega-consistency and states the strengthening used here.

The spectral-undecidability paragraph correctly concerns a family of infinite-system decisions, not finite-matrix diagonalization or this particular Yang–Mills theory. [Cubitt–Perez-Garcia–Wolf's full paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04573), Theorem 3, supplies the stronger restrictions: translation-invariant nearest-neighbor interactions on a two-dimensional lattice, with fixed finite local dimension. The document's less detailed description is accurate.
